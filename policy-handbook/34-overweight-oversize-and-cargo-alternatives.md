# 34 — Overweight, oversize, and cargo alternatives

## Scope and customer language

Retrieve for “bag is 35 kilos,” “too big for the belt,” “will not fit the sizer,” “send it as cargo,” or “they refused my suitcase.” A passenger bag has a hard ceiling even when the traveller offers to pay.

## Required action

A checked piece over 32 kg, or with length plus width plus height over 203 cm, is not accepted at the passenger bag drop. Weight of 23 to 32 kg, or size of 159 to 203 cm, can travel only after the overweight or oversize fee in the excess-baggage rule is paid. Explain the measured weight and size, then offer a lawful alternative: move weight into another piece that stays inside the limit, leave items behind, or book the piece as cargo through the cargo desk on a service that accepts it. Cargo may leave on a different flight and has its own acceptance and price.

If the passenger will not repack, pay, or use cargo, the item stays behind and the passenger may still travel. Do not tell staff to load an overweight bag as a favour, and do not invent a fine beyond the published fee. A bag refused for weight is not a denied-boarding compensation case.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate a mobility aid, musical instrument, or sporting item that has its own acceptance rule, and any bag already tagged above the limit.

**Compliant:** “At 35 kg this cannot travel as a passenger bag. You can move items out until it is 32 kg or less, or ask the cargo desk whether it can go as freight.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Pay a little extra and any weight is allowed.”

**Authority:** carrier checked-baggage acceptance limits and cargo booking rules.
