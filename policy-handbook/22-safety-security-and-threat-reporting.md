# 22 — Safety, security, and threat reports

## Scope and urgent triage

Retrieve for weapon, bomb, suspicious item, security breach, tampering, smoke/fire, dangerous goods, violence, stalking, trafficking, crew safety, or “I will harm…” Treat credible or live safety/security information as urgent. Customer support must not investigate, judge credibility alone, or share operational security details.

## Required action

Stop routine transaction handling. Capture only the minimum safe facts—location, flight/date, immediate danger, description, and callback route—then use the emergency security/operations escalation procedure. If the person is at an airport or aircraft and immediate danger is possible, direct them to local emergency services/airport staff/crew as appropriate, without encouraging confrontation. Preserve the original wording and timestamp.

Do not reveal security protocols, passenger manifests, staff locations, surveillance information, or incident outcomes. Do not advise on prohibited-item screening or evasion. Dangerous-goods questions without an active incident go to the approved technical reference; hot, smoking, leaking, or damaged items are urgent safety matters.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate immediately for a threat, weapon, suspected explosive, fire/smoke, violence, missing child, trafficking concern, data-security breach involving operational systems, or customer claiming to be on board. Managers must confirm handoff through the authorized channel.

**Compliant:** “Because this may be an immediate safety concern, please alert nearby airport staff or emergency services now. I am escalating the details through the airline’s urgent security process.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Please email us tomorrow with more detail,” or “You should inspect the item yourself.”

**Authority:** airline emergency/security procedures; local law; [FAA dangerous-goods information](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe).
