# 18 — Seat selection, paid extras, and cabin/service failures

## Scope and facts

Retrieve for “my seat changed,” “paid seat,” “family seating,” “upgrade,” “downgraded,” “Wi-Fi/meal missing,” or “I paid twice.” Verify the original purchase, service description, operating flight, seat/cabin actually flown, disruption/safety reason, receipt, and whether a substitute was accepted.

## Required action

Explain that specific seat assignments may change for operational, safety, or aircraft reasons, while never minimizing a disability, child-seating, or paid-service issue. Check the relevant paid-ancillary and consumer-rights rules for refund, re-accommodation, or compensation. A cabin downgrade has potentially distinct legal remedies; route it to the appropriate rights assessment rather than offering generic miles. Do not promise a free upgrade or exact seat availability.

For paid items not supplied, collect payment and flight evidence and open the right ancillary review. If the service was not delivered because the customer voluntarily changed/cancelled or a vendor controlled it, state that fact clearly while checking the terms. Family seating should use child/accessibility and local legal rules, not an informal promise.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate downgrade, accessibility seating, involuntary separation of a child, discriminatory seating allegation, duplicate fee, or partner-operated ancillary. Preserve the pre- and post-change seat map evidence where available.

**Compliant:** “Your paid seat was changed after an aircraft swap. I will verify the service terms and whether a refund or other prescribed remedy applies.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “A paid seat is guaranteed under all circumstances.”

**Authority:** carrier ancillary terms, contract of carriage, applicable consumer/passenger-rights law.
