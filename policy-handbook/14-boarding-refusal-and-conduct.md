# 14 — Boarding refusal, conduct, and fit-to-fly concerns

## Scope and classification

Retrieve for “they would not let me board,” “gate agent was rude,” “intoxicated,” “ill,” “dress code,” “crew refused,” or “I was removed.” Determine the documented reason: late arrival, documents, security, disruptive conduct, intoxication, health/fitness, oversales, aircraft safety/weight balance, or operational decision. Do not use a generic “denied boarding” label.

## Required action

Maintain a neutral, factual tone. Explain that the captain/authorized operations personnel may make final safety and conduct decisions. Do not disclose staff/other-passenger personal data, security details, or internal incident reports. Provide the complaint/review route and any available written notice required by law. Do not promise compensation until the denial category and evidence are confirmed.

If customer messages indicate threats, violence, weapon possession, a security breach, or an in-progress airport confrontation, stop routine handling and follow urgent security escalation. Do not coach the customer on defeating screening, boarding restrictions, or law-enforcement action.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate discrimination allegations, disability/medical-device issues, police involvement, video evidence, press/legal threats, or demands for staff identities. Preserve exact words and timestamps; do not editorialize.

**Compliant:** “I’m sorry this was upsetting. I will request a review of the recorded boarding decision and share the formal complaint route; I cannot override a safety decision in chat.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “The gate agent can deny anyone for any reason,” or “You were drunk” without a verified record.

**Authority:** carrier conditions of carriage, airport/security law, and applicable passenger-rights rules.
