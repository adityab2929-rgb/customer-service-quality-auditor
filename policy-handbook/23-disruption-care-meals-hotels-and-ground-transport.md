# 23 — Disruption care: meals, hotels, and ground transport

## Scope and decision factors

Retrieve for “hotel,” “meal voucher,” “taxi,” “overnight,” “stranded,” or “who pays.” Eligibility is not universal. Verify cause, itinerary/jurisdiction, expected delay, time of day, passenger location, ability to leave airport, carrier availability, prepaid arrangements, and any special needs. A weather or ATC cause may change compensation analysis but does not remove the need to check published care obligations.

## Required action

Consult the live disruption matrix before promising a hotel, meal, transport, reimbursement, or expense cap. Tell the customer whether the carrier is arranging assistance directly, authorizing reasonable self-arranged costs, or assessing a claim later; give the approved receipt and spending guidance. Do not advise a passenger to book luxury accommodation or make nonrefundable purchases unless explicitly authorized.

For EU261-covered cases, care obligations may apply during qualifying lengthy delays/cancellations even where extraordinary circumstances may defeat monetary compensation. For other journeys, use the carrier’s contract/customer commitment and local law. Check immigration, curfew, age, accessibility, and baggage constraints before suggesting an overnight move.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate a stranded minor, lack of accessible accommodation, unsafe late-night situation, unable-to-leave-airport customer, mass disruption, or a customer who already incurred substantial expenses. Log vouchers offered and refused.

**Compliant:** “I’m checking the current disruption record and applicable care rules before I confirm whether we can arrange or reimburse overnight accommodation and transport.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Weather means we never provide any assistance.”

**Authority:** carrier disruption plan; [EU Regulation 261/2004](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2004/261/oj/eng).
