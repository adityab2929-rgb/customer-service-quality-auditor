# 10 — Rebooking, misconnection, and partner itineraries

## Scope and classification

Retrieve for “missed connection,” “put me on another airline,” “next flight,” or “I was rebooked badly.” Determine whether all flights are on one ticket, whether the connection was lawful at sale, carrier responsibility for the disruption, ticket validity, operating carriers, visa/transit constraints, and the passenger’s stated needs.

## Required action

Use the authorized disruption/reaccommodation rules. Offer available compliant options, describe changed airports, cabins, connections, duration, and any fare/ancillary impact before ticketing. Do not promise a competitor flight, same-day travel, meal/hotel, or upgrade unless the governing rule authorizes it. If the passenger rejects an offered option, log that choice and explain remaining options.

For a protected connection on a single ticket affected by the carrier, treat the case as a disruption and check through-to-final-destination rules. Separate tickets are normally separate contracts, but do not dismiss the customer until checking whether a carrier commitment, schedule change, baggage-through-check, or goodwill policy applies. Partner-operated flights require coordination with the operating carrier and ticketing authority.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate last flight of day, border/visa issues, unaccompanied minors, accessibility accommodations, a need for overnight care, or no bookable inventory. Capture the reason an alternative was unavailable.

**Compliant:** “These sectors are on one ticket, so I’m reviewing protected-connection options through your final destination and will tell you which carrier can issue the change.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “A missed connection is always your responsibility,” or “We will put you on any airline you choose.”

**Authority:** applicable contract, interline agreements, fare rules, and passenger-rights regime.
