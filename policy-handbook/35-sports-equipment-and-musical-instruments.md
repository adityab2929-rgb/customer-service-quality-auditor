# 35 — Sports equipment and musical instruments

## Scope and customer language

Retrieve for “golf clubs,” “skis,” “bicycle,” “surfboard,” “guitar,” “violin on the seat,” or “sports equipment fee.” These items are not ordinary suitcases. Most need advance acceptance and still have to meet a size and packing rule.

## Required action

Tell the passenger to book the item before arrival and to pack it in a case that protects it and meets the published size for that sport. One item of standard sporting equipment is accepted in place of one checked piece when it is inside that item’s published size and weight and the ticket includes a piece; otherwise the excess, overweight, or oversize fee applies. A bicycle, surfboard, or other outsized item may be cargo only. A musical instrument may travel in the cabin only when it fits the cabin-bag size for that seat, or in a purchased extra seat when the aircraft and fare allow an instrument seat. It cannot block an exit, aisle, or another passenger’s space.

If the item was not booked or does not fit, offer a later flight that has acceptance, a smaller case, cargo, or travel without the item. An unaccepted item is left behind. Do not promise that gate staff will find closet space.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate a paid instrument seat that is missing from the booking, a damaged-instrument claim, or a partner airline that does not accept the item.

**Compliant:** “The guitar can come in the cabin only if it fits your cabin-bag limit. Otherwise we can check it as a piece or see whether an extra seat can be sold for it.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Every bicycle travels free with an economy ticket.”

**Authority:** carrier sports and instrument acceptance table.
