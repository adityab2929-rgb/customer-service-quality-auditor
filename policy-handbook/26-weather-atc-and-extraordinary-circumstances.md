# 26 — Weather, ATC, strikes, and extraordinary-circumstances claims

## Scope and evidence discipline

Retrieve for “weather was fine,” “ATC delay,” “strike,” “mechanical,” “not your fault,” or “extraordinary circumstance.” Cause labels have legal and contractual consequences; use only verified operations records. A delay may have multiple causes. Front-line staff must not decide complex compensation eligibility from a generic weather code.

## Required action

Explain the recorded operational cause in neutral language and state that rebooking/refund/care and compensation can have different tests. Even when an event is outside the carrier’s control, check duties to provide accurate information, reroute, assist, or refund under the applicable contract/law. Do not say “weather means nothing is owed” or “mechanical issues always qualify” without the authorized assessment.

For EU261, extraordinary circumstances may affect compensation but do not automatically remove all cancellation/delay assistance obligations; individual facts and reasonable-measures analysis matter. Do not promise or deny EU compensation before the specialized assessment. Similar care/refund duties elsewhere must be assessed under the relevant jurisdiction.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate disputed cause, labor action, repeated cancellations, crew/maintenance allegations, customer evidence contradicting operations, group disruption, and regulator/legal request. Preserve source timestamp and any later cause update.

**Compliant:** “Operations currently records an air-traffic-control restriction. I can arrange the applicable travel option now; the compensation review needs the final cause assessment.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Weather always cancels all rights.”

**Authority:** [EU261 text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2004/261/oj/eng), operating-carrier record, contract/customer-service plan.
