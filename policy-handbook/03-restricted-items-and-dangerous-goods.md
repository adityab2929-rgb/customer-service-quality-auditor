# 03 — Restricted items, batteries, and dangerous goods

## Scope and customer language

Retrieve for batteries, power banks, vapes, e-cigarettes, smart bags, dry ice, aerosols, fuel, camping stove, ammunition, medical oxygen, wheelchair batteries, “can I pack this,” or a security-screening refusal. Dangerous-goods rules depend on item type, quantity, battery watt-hours/lithium content, packaging, location in baggage, carrier approval, and route. Security rules may be stricter.

## Required support response

Do not guess and do not advise a customer to conceal, discharge, dismantle, mail, or abandon an item. Direct them to the current approved dangerous-goods reference and security authority for their itinerary; collect item name, manufacturer, battery specification, and whether installed or spare. Explain the core rule plainly: spare lithium batteries, power banks, and electronic cigarettes must not go in checked baggage; if cabin baggage is gate-checked, these items must be removed and kept accessible in the cabin. Damaged, defective, or recalled lithium batteries must not travel.

For battery-powered mobility devices, notify the accessibility/dangerous-goods desk and obtain required battery information; never deny or promise carriage in chat. Some larger lithium-ion batteries require airline approval, while some items are prohibited. Airline and international limits can differ.

## Safety escalation and examples

Immediately route smoke, heat, leakage, an undeclared hazardous item, threats, or a customer currently at an airport to airport/operations safety procedures. Do not handle a live safety incident as a routine complaint.

**Compliant:** “A power bank is a spare lithium battery, so it cannot be placed in checked baggage. Please keep it in your cabin bag and let us verify its rating if it is unusually large.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “All batteries are fine in checked luggage.”

**Authority:** [FAA PackSafe battery rules](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/airline-passengers-and-batteries), [FAA lithium-baggage guidance](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/lithium-batteries-baggage).
