# 24 — Travel credits, vouchers, and unused ticket value

## Scope and customer language

Retrieve for “voucher,” “credit expired,” “use my credit,” “flight coupon,” “refund instead,” or “transfer credit.” Identify the source: voluntary cancellation, airline disruption, goodwill, promotional award, or unused ticket; then verify owner, value/currency, issue and expiry dates, restrictions, and ticket/credit number through approved authentication.

## Required action

Explain what the credit is, how it differs from a cash/card refund, its expiry and booking/travel-by rules, transferability, eligible products, currency/fare-difference treatment, and whether it can be combined. Use the written terms that applied when issued. Do not call a credit “refundable” or “cash equivalent” unless the terms legally make it so. Do not override expiration, change ownership, or reissue value outside authority.

If an airline cancellation or significant change creates a refund right, offer the legally/contractually required refund path before or alongside any voluntary credit option. Document an informed customer choice. For a system error or credited amount dispute, collect evidence and route to ticketing/finance; never tell a customer to book a new fare and “sort it out later.”

## Escalation and examples

Escalate expired credit where a service failure is alleged, insolvency/chargeback, a deceased traveler, partner/agency booking, duplicate credits, or legal/regulator complaint. Preserve the terms and issuance message.

**Compliant:** “This is a travel credit, not automatically a refund. I’ll verify its original terms and also check whether your airline-initiated cancellation qualifies for a refund option.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Credits never expire,” or “You must accept a voucher.”

**Authority:** voucher terms, fare rules, contract of carriage, applicable refund law.
