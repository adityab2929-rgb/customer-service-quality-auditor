# 27 — Fare rules, no-shows, coupons, and ticket validity

## Scope and key terms

Retrieve for “missed my flight,” “use return only,” “coupon invalid,” “ticket expired,” “hidden city,” “change after no-show,” or “why repriced.” Verify ticket status for every coupon, fare rule, booked versus flown sequence, check-in/boarding history, and whether a disruption caused the missed flight. Do not assume that a customer was a no-show when an airport/system failure may be involved.

## Required action

Explain the applicable fare’s no-show, sequence-of-use, validity, and reissue conditions in readable language. Before any voluntary reissue, state the effect on unused segments, fare difference, fees, and ticket validity. Do not advise customers to intentionally skip legs, manipulate itinerary use, or violate the contract. If the carrier caused a disruption, route to involuntary reaccommodation rather than applying voluntary no-show rules blindly.

For an alleged airport queue, app outage, inaccessible service, or agent error, preserve evidence and route for review. Do not promise restoration of a cancelled segment without inventory and ticketing authorization.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate forced missed connection, hospital/emergency documentation, infant/minor itinerary, agency ticket, partner flight, duplicate booking, fraud suspicion, or contract interpretation dispute. Keep the original fare rule version used for the decision.

**Compliant:** “I’ll first verify whether the missed segment resulted from an airline disruption. If it did not, I’ll quote the ticket’s no-show and remaining-coupon rules before you choose a reissue.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Missing any flight always voids the whole ticket.”

**Authority:** fare rule, contract of carriage, ticketing procedures, applicable law.
