# 38 — Lithium batteries, power banks, and packing location

## Scope and customer language

Retrieve for “power bank,” “spare battery,” “laptop in checked baggage,” “Wh rating,” “e-cigarette,” or “where do I put the battery.” The passenger’s duty is to keep spare lithium batteries where cabin crew can reach them.

## Required action

Spare lithium batteries, power banks, and e-cigarettes travel in the cabin, not in checked baggage. A power bank is a spare battery even when it is switched off. Installed batteries in a laptop, phone, or camera may stay inside the device. Each spare battery at or below 100 watt-hours is allowed in the cabin within the carrier’s quantity. A battery above 100 and up to 160 watt-hours needs airline approval before travel and is limited to two spares. A battery above 160 watt-hours, or any damaged, swollen, recalled, or unprotected battery, does not travel.

If the passenger packed a power bank in a suitcase, they must remove it before check-in. If the cabin bag will be gate-checked, they must take the power bank out and carry it. If the battery is over the limit or damaged, the alternatives are a smaller battery, shipment by an approved dangerous-goods cargo service, or travel without it. The bag can be refused, and an undeclared or concealed lithium battery can bring a security or dangerous-goods penalty. Do not tell the passenger to hide a battery in checked clothing.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate a hot, smoking, or leaking battery, a mobility-device battery, or a passenger already at the aircraft door.

**Compliant:** “The power bank has to come out of the checked bag and travel with you in the cabin. If it is over 100 watt-hours, I need to check whether this ticket allows it.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Turn the power bank off and it can go in the hold.”

**Authority:** [FAA PackSafe battery rules](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/airline-passengers-and-batteries).
