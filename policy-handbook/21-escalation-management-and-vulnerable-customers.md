# 21 — Escalation, de-escalation, and vulnerable customers

## Scope and indicators

Retrieve for “supervisor,” repeated contacts, abusive language, distress, self-harm, emergency, media, lawyer, regulator, or complex unresolved complaint. An escalation is not a reason to end help. Distinguish dissatisfaction and profanity from a credible immediate safety threat.

## Required action

Use calm, plain language: acknowledge, summarize the problem, state the next verifiable action, owner, and timeframe. Set respectful boundaries for abusive conduct without retaliation: continue assistance where safely possible and move to a safe approved channel if necessary. Do not promise a supervisor callback, payout, or decision unless the workflow authorizes it.

Give priority routing for stranded passengers, minors, disability/accessibility needs, medical/welfare issues, domestic violence/trafficking indicators, and customers who may be unable to navigate standard processes. Minimize collection of sensitive information and avoid diagnosing or interrogating. If a customer expresses imminent self-harm or danger, follow emergency escalation immediately and use local emergency guidance; do not attempt counseling.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate threats, stalking/doxxing, credible safety/security issues, discrimination, legal/media/regulatory contact, a high-value systemic failure, and repeat contacts after missed commitments. Log exact words, actions, and handoff—not labels such as “difficult customer.”

**Compliant:** “I hear that this has been exhausting. I cannot change the safety decision in chat, but I can submit the review now and tell you the next update point.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Calm down or we will cancel your ticket.”

**Authority:** crisis, safeguarding, conduct, and complaint-management procedures.
