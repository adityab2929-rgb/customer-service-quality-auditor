# 16 — Medical travel, pregnancy, and medication concerns

## Scope and safe boundaries

Retrieve for pregnancy, recent surgery, oxygen, medication, “fit to fly,” contagious illness, or medical clearance. Support staff must not diagnose, give treatment, interpret clinical records, or guarantee medical suitability for flight. The passenger’s clinician and applicable public-health/border rules control health advice.

## Required action

Provide current carrier policy and refer the customer to their clinician where a fitness-to-fly certificate or airline medical clearance is required. Explain document timing/form requirements without evaluating the medical content. For medication, advise keeping essential medication accessible in cabin baggage where permitted and checking destination prescription/import rules through official sources. Do not advise packing prohibited items or sharps without the relevant security/dangerous-goods process.

For pregnancy, state only the published carrier limits and documentation conditions; do not assume a cutoff is universal or use pregnancy to deny travel absent policy/medical process. For oxygen, CPAP, portable concentrators, or mobility devices, route to medical/accessibility and dangerous-goods review. A customer reporting acute symptoms, a medical emergency, or infectious-disease risk at the airport must be sent to the emergency/airport health process, not ticket changes.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate clearance disputes, denied boarding for health reasons, privacy concerns, in-flight medical events, and any request to override a safety rule. Handle medical documents as sensitive data.

**Compliant:** “I can confirm the carrier’s current documentation process, but your clinician must advise whether you are fit to travel. The specialist will review the portable-device requirements.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “It is medically safe for you to fly,” or “Pregnant customers cannot fly.”

**Authority:** carrier medical policy, official health/border guidance, accessibility and dangerous-goods rules.
