# 37 — Sharp objects, tools, and sporting blades

## Scope and customer language

Retrieve for “knife,” “scissors,” “razor,” “tools,” “screwdriver,” “hiking pole,” “bat,” “ice skates,” or “can this go in my backpack.” Cabin carriage is refused when the item can be used as a weapon, even if the passenger uses it for work or sport.

## Required action

Tell the passenger that knives, box cutters, scissors with blades over the departure airport’s published length, tools, baseball bats, golf clubs, hiking poles, and ice skates do not travel in the cabin. A disposable razor and the published small scissors or nail file may travel in the cabin only when that airport allows them. Any sharp item that is lawful to carry at all must be sheathed or wrapped and placed in checked baggage, not in a pocket or cabin bag.

If the passenger still has the item at the airport, the alternatives are to place it in a checked bag before bag drop closes, leave it with someone not travelling, surrender it at security, or move to a later flight after packing it lawfully. Security may confiscate a cabin item without airline compensation. Do not suggest packing it where screening is less likely to look. An attempt to carry a prohibited blade into a secure area can also bring an airport or police response.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate a tool the passenger needs for work at the destination, a religious or ceremonial blade, or any item security has already held.

**Compliant:** “The folding knife cannot ride in your backpack. If you are still outside bag drop, it can go in a checked bag, wrapped, or you can leave it behind.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Put the knife in your pocket and walk through.”

**Authority:** departure-airport prohibited-items list; [TSA What Can I Bring](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/all).
