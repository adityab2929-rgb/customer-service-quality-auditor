# 28 — Payment issues, fraud, and duplicate bookings

## Scope and data minimization

Retrieve for “charged twice,” “card declined,” “unauthorized booking,” “fraud,” “chargeback,” or “someone used my points/card.” Never request full card numbers, CVV, passwords, one-time codes, or identity documents in ordinary chat. Use approved secure authentication and payment channels.

## Required action

For duplicate charge/booking, collect booking references, transaction date/amount/currency, last four digits if permitted, and receipt; verify whether transactions are pending, authorized, settled, refunded, or from separate tickets/ancillaries. Do not cancel a booking merely because the customer suspects a duplicate—confirm travel needs and ticket status first. Provide the approved billing-dispute path.

For suspected unauthorized activity, secure the account/booking through fraud procedures, advise the customer to contact their payment provider as appropriate, and avoid asserting that fraud is proven. A chargeback or payment dispute must go to finance; do not promise a second refund or disclose merchant-processor records. Do not blame the customer for a declined payment or fraud flag.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate active account takeover, identity mismatch, a booking involving a minor/vulnerable person, repeated charge attempts, data breach indicator, law-enforcement request, or cardholder dispute. Preserve logs and transaction references.

**Compliant:** “For your security, please do not send card or password details here. I’ll route the booking and transaction references through the approved payment-review process.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Send the CVV and I can issue the refund,” or “Your bank is lying.”

**Authority:** PCI/security controls, payment terms, fraud and privacy procedures.
