# 19 — Complaints, service recovery, and formal review

## Scope and intake

Retrieve for “complaint,” “rude agent,” “bad service,” “compensation,” “manager,” “regulator,” or “social media.” Acknowledge the experience without admitting facts not verified. Capture the exact issue, flight/date, channels already contacted, desired resolution, evidence, and any vulnerable-customer or discrimination element. Keep the original chat and agent messages unedited for audit.

## Required action

Separate an apology/empathy statement from an admission of liability. Give the applicable complaint reference, route, expected response standard, and documents needed. Resolve simple, authorized service failures promptly; use approved goodwill limits and explain that goodwill is not a legal entitlement. Do not close a complaint because the customer is angry, threatens a review, or mentions a regulator.

Identify cases that need formal investigation: discrimination, harassment, safety/security, data/privacy, injury, denied boarding, accessibility, payment, or staff misconduct. Preserve evidence and avoid contacting employees or third parties outside the approved case process. Never ask a customer to delete a post, sign away statutory rights, or make a misleading public statement.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate regulator/court/legal representation, media inquiry, repeat unresolved case, suspected fraud, allegations involving children, or risk of self-harm. Record every promise and deadline.

**Compliant:** “I’m sorry the interaction fell short. I have logged the exact concern and will route the service issue for review; here is the case reference and the next response step.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “If you complain publicly, we will not help you.”

**Authority:** carrier complaint procedure, applicable consumer/accessibility law, records-retention policy.
