# 12 — Ticket changes, corrections, and transfer prevention

## Scope and evidence

Retrieve for “change flight,” “wrong date,” “spelling mistake,” “name change,” “sell/give ticket,” or “new passport name.” Verify ticket status, fare family, change deadline, booking channel, itinerary, traveler identity, and requested edit. A name correction is not necessarily a transferable ticket or a passenger substitution.

## Required action

Read the fare rule and approved name-correction policy before repricing or changing. Explain fare difference, change fee if applicable, currency, ticket validity, no-show consequences, and whether the customer needs to contact the booking agent. For correction requests, collect only necessary evidence and use secure document workflow; do not request passport images in an unsecured chat. Do not alter a passenger name where the request creates a different traveler without authorized exception.

When a schedule disruption caused by the airline is the actual reason, switch to the disruption/rebooking policy rather than collecting a voluntary change fee. Never promise a “free correction” or “no fare difference” without checking policy and inventory.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate international tickets, partner itineraries, married/divorced name evidence, suspected fraud, duplicate PNR, or a change after check-in. Sensitive identity discrepancies should be resolved before travel but agents must not give immigration-document advice.

**Compliant:** “I can check whether this is an allowed spelling correction or a new passenger request. I’ll confirm the fare-rule impact before any ticket is changed.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Tickets can always be transferred to a friend.”

**Authority:** carrier contract/fare rules; identity, border, and security requirements.
