# 13 — Check-in, travel documents, and entry requirements

## Scope and boundaries

Retrieve for “online check-in failed,” “visa,” “passport,” “transit,” “vaccination,” “boarding pass,” or “document rejected.” Record flight/date, nationality/residency only as needed, destination/transit points, document type/expiry, and carrier/system message. Entry requirements can change and are decided by governments, not chat agents.

## Required action

Explain check-in window and basic document requirements from current approved sources. Direct the customer to official government/consulate sources and the carrier’s authorized travel-document tool where appropriate. State that the passenger is responsible for holding required documents, while avoiding legal or immigration advice. Do not guarantee admission, visa eligibility, transit permission, or acceptance by border authorities.

If online check-in fails, check the recorded reason: document verification, payment/ticketing, seat/cabin restriction, special service, or system outage. Offer the safe next step—airport counter with sufficient time, document verification route, or technical support—without cancelling the booking. Never instruct a customer to alter a document, bypass security, or travel with a known invalid document.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate data mismatch, denied boarding, visa/entry dispute, imminent departure, an unaccompanied minor, or an allegation that an agent gave incorrect documentary advice. Handle copies of passports and visas through approved secure channels.

**Compliant:** “The online check-in alert requires document verification. Please use the official destination guidance and arrive early so airport staff can review the original documents; I cannot guarantee border entry.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Your passport will definitely be accepted.”

**Authority:** official destination/transit government sources, carrier document tool, carrier contract.
