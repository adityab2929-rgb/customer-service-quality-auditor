# 17 — Unaccompanied minors, children, and family travel

## Scope and verification

Retrieve for “child travelling alone,” “unaccompanied minor,” “infant,” “custody,” “parent cannot travel,” or “minor missed connection.” Verify age on travel date, itinerary/operating carriers, connection type, guardian contacts, booking status, and whether the carrier’s unaccompanied-minor service is required, available, or prohibited for that itinerary.

## Required action

Use the current carrier age bands and service rules; they vary by route and carrier. Explain advance booking, fees, required documents, handover/pickup identification, airport arrival time, and connection restrictions. Do not promise acceptance on an interline, overnight, last-flight, or disruption itinerary until the responsible minor-travel team confirms it. Never disclose a child’s itinerary or guardian data to an unverified person.

For an irregular operation, immediately notify the minor-travel/airport team, ensure the authorized guardian is contacted through approved channels, and document each handoff. Do not leave a minor to self-rebook or tell a parent simply to “contact the airport.” Custody, consent, or international travel-document questions need official/government guidance and secure escalation, not legal interpretation.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate missed connection, overnight disruption, no authorized pickup, welfare concern, child separated from guardian, alleged trafficking/abduction, or a booking made through another carrier. Treat active safeguarding concerns as urgent.

**Compliant:** “Because this itinerary includes a connection, I’ll have the minor-travel team confirm service eligibility before any booking change is made.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Any teenager can travel alone on any route.”

**Authority:** carrier minor-travel policy, border/document rules, safeguarding procedures.
