# 11 — Refunds, method of payment, and chargebacks

## Scope and classification

Retrieve for “refund,” “credit,” “cash,” “refund status,” “chargeback,” or “paid by card/agency.” Identify the refund basis: voluntary cancellation, airline cancellation/significant change, unused ancillary service, duplicate charge, denied boarding, or baggage fee. Verify ticket/EMD status, payment tender, purchaser, booking channel, and whether travel or an alternative was accepted.

## Required action

Explain the eligibility basis and expected processing path before submitting. A refund due under law or contract must be handled through the required method and timeframe; do not substitute a voucher without the customer’s informed agreement where a refund is owed. Refund to original form of payment unless the governing rule and authorization permit another method. Do not quote a timeline from memory—use the current finance workflow.

For agency bookings, identify whether the airline can process the refund directly or must authorize the agency. Preserve the payment evidence and avoid duplicating a refund. A chargeback notice, fraud allegation, or already-refunded transaction goes to payments specialists; do not ask for card numbers or sensitive authentication data in chat.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate partial flown tickets, multiple tender, deceased passenger, insolvency, regulator complaint, duplicate refund risk, or disputed significant-change finding. Quality review should compare the agent’s wording with the actual ticket/refund record.

**Compliant:** “I’ll confirm whether this is an airline-initiated refund or a voluntary cancellation, then submit the correct method-of-payment request or explain any fare-rule limitation.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “All refunds take the same number of days,” or “A travel credit is the same as a refund.”

**Authority:** [US DOT refund guidance](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), applicable fare/contract and local law.
