# 25 — Travel-agent, online-agency, codeshare, and package bookings

## Scope and ownership

Retrieve for “I booked through…,” “agency will not help,” “package holiday,” “codeshare,” “who refunds,” or “airline says contact agent.” Verify ticket stock/issuer, booking owner, operating carrier, marketing carrier, package organizer, flight coupon status, and the exact request. Customers should not be bounced between parties without a clear reason and next action.

## Required action

State what the airline can do directly—such as operational status, certain day-of-travel assistance, or authorized irregular-operation rebooking—and what must be processed by the ticketing agent or package organizer. Provide a specific contact path and the information the customer should give them. Do not alter or cancel agency-controlled tickets without authority, but do not deny a statutory airline obligation merely because an intermediary sold the ticket.

Codeshare and interline cases require the operating carrier’s service facts and the ticketing carrier’s reissue/refund authority. Package-travel rights may differ from flight-only rights; do not interpret package law without specialist routing. Maintain empathy and ownership of the next supportable step.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate an airline-cancelled flight where agency support is unavailable, a customer stranded during travel, ticketing authority conflict, duplicate rebooking, schedule change, refund dispute, or package organizer failure. Preserve proof of all referrals.

**Compliant:** “The agency issued this ticket, so it controls a voluntary change. Because the operating flight was cancelled, I’m checking what assistance we can provide now and what authorization the agency needs.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “You booked elsewhere, so the airline has no responsibility.”

**Authority:** ticketing agreement, contract of carriage, applicable package/consumer law.
