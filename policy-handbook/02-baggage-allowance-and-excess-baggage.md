# 02 — Baggage allowance, excess, and oversized items

## Scope and likely phrases

Retrieve for “how many bags,” “carry-on,” “overweight,” “oversize,” “sports equipment,” “I paid at the airport,” or “the agent charged me twice.” Baggage allowance is ticket- and itinerary-specific. Confirm the operating carrier, fare brand/cabin, route, ticket issue date, loyalty status, and whether the item is checked, cabin, sports, musical, medical, or infant equipment. A marketing carrier’s website may not control a partner-operated flight.

## Required action

Quote the applicable published allowance and fee rule, including dimensions, weight, piece count, currency, payment/refund conditions, and partner-carrier restrictions. Explain that airport acceptance can depend on aircraft capacity, loading, safety, and local handling limits. Never guarantee acceptance of an oversize item, cabin bag, or connecting-carrier item without confirmation.

If a customer alleges a duplicate or incorrect fee, collect receipt/EMD, bag tag, itinerary, payment evidence, and flight details; open the approved billing review rather than refusing solely because payment occurred at the airport. For medical/assistive equipment, route to accessibility procedures, not ordinary excess-baggage rules. Route sports, instruments, weapons, perishables, and fragile/high-value items to their specialist policies.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate a disputed fee, inconsistent quoted allowance, involuntary gate check, or partner itinerary where a published rule may have been misapplied. Do not tell the customer to repack dangerous goods at the counter; use the dangerous-goods policy.

**Compliant:** “Your ticket shows a partner-operated second sector, so I’ll check that carrier’s allowance before confirming whether the ski bag fee applies.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “One checked bag is free on every international ticket,” or “Pay first and we will definitely refund it later.”

**Authority:** carrier contract/fare rules; [US DOT baggage guidance](https://www.transportation.gov/lost-delayed-or-damaged-baggage).
