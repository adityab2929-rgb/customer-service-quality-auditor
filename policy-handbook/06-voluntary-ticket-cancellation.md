# 06 — Voluntary passenger cancellations

## Scope and facts to verify

Retrieve for “I cannot travel,” “cancel my ticket,” “change of plans,” “bereavement,” “illness,” or “refund my nonrefundable ticket.” Verify who booked, ticket number, fare conditions, purchase channel, departure status, all coupon status, ancillary products, and jurisdiction. A request initiated by the passenger is not automatically an airline cancellation.

## Required action

Read the fare rule and ticketing-channel conditions before cancellation. Explain the available outcome in plain terms: refundable amount, credit/voucher, change/cancel fee if permitted, forfeiture/no-show consequence, validity, and any documented exception process. State whether action must occur before scheduled departure. Do not cancel a live reservation merely to quote an option; obtain explicit consent and document it.

If the booking was made through an agency, online travel agency, employer, or package provider, explain whether that seller controls the ticket or refund handling and supply the correct contact route. Do not send the customer between parties without identifying the record owner and what the airline can do. For illness, death, military orders, or duplicate booking, use a documented exception workflow and protect sensitive documents.

## Escalation and examples

Escalate where law creates a cooling-off right, an airline schedule change may convert the request into an involuntary refund case, a chargeback is threatened, or fare rules are unclear. Never describe a voucher as a refund.

**Compliant:** “Your request is voluntary. I will check the fare conditions and the booking owner, then I can confirm whether the unused value is refundable or eligible for credit before you choose.”

**Potentially non-compliant:** “Nonrefundable means there are never any options.”

**Authority:** applicable contract of carriage, fare rule, ticketing terms, and local consumer law.
